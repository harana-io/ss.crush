Dexterity taking 4 boosts is only possible with Dex as the class key attribute — so **the key-attribute question is answered: Dexterity.** That's the right call, and it matches what I'd have recommended.

**What Str 14 / Wis 8 buys and costs.** Strength is your *only* damage modifier — the Scoundrel racket doesn't add Dex to damage the way Thief does — so Str 14 is +2 on every melee Strike, a meaningful chunk of a 1d6 weapon. You pay for it with Perception and Will at **+4** instead of +5. That matters less than it looks: **Surprise Attack** wants you rolling Stealth (+7) or Deception (+5) for initiative anyway, so your Perception rarely gets rolled for initiative at all. Good trade.

Attack = Dex +4 · Prof 3 (finesse weapons use Dex). Melee damage = weapon die + Str +2.

> **Watch the finesse trait.** Only *finesse* lets you attack with Dexterity; *agile* just reduces the multiple-attack penalty. The **sap** (1d6 B) is agile + nonlethal but **not finesse**, so it would attack at Str +2 → **+5**. **Monkey's Fist** costs the same 1 sp, deals the same 1d6 B, and *is* finesse → **+7**. Take the Monkey's Fist. Both carry sneak attack (agile *or* finesse qualifies).

**Sneak Attack 1d6** (precision) — against an **off-guard** target, with an agile or finesse melee weapon, an agile/finesse unarmed attack, or **any** ranged attack. All four qualify.

## The Scoundrel Engine

1. **Feint** (1 action, Deception **+5** vs. their Perception DC) → target is **off-guard against your melee attacks** until the end of your next turn. On a **crit**, off-guard against *everyone's* melee attacks.
2. Wielding an agile or finesse melee weapon, a successful Feint lets you **Step as a free action** — into flank, or out of a reach threat, free.
3. **Strike** the off-guard target → **1d6+2 plus 1d6 sneak attack**.

**Surprise Attack** covers round one: roll **Deception or Stealth for initiative** and every creature that hasn't acted is off-guard to you. Default to **Stealth (+7)**; roll Deception when you're already mid-conversation with the people about to become a problem.

Deception at +5 is the soft spot. Put your **level 2 skill increase into Deception** (expert, +7) — rogues get a skill increase at level 2, earlier than most classes.

**One note on Lie to Me** — it's purely *defensive*. It protects you from being conned; it does nothing for Feint or for your own lies. If you were hoping for something that boosts Feint, **Bon Mot** (Diplomacy vs. Will → −2 to their Perception *and* Will) is the one — and you get another skill feat at level 2, so you can just take it then.

### Why Nature

Per *Player Core* p. 231, Nature covers Recall Knowledge on **animals, beasts, fey, and plants** — the creature types that dominate low-level encounters — plus **geography, weather, and the environment**. That pairs directly with your tracking kit: Survival does the *doing* (Track, Sense Direction, Subsist), Nature does the *knowing*. Your marks themselves are humanoids, which is **Society**, already trained at +3.

**Watch for the Medicine hole.** Treat Wounds is the party's only reliable out-of-combat healing and it **requires trained in Medicine** — untrained, the action doesn't exist. If it turns out nobody in the party has it, that's the gap to fix, and a rogue skill increase can cover it as early as level 2. Medicine's forensic-examination use (how a body was injured or killed) works **untrained**, so you have that regardless.

### Taking marks alive

You don't strictly need a nonlethal weapon. **Nonlethal Attacks** (*Player Core* p. 407): you can make any attack nonlethal at a **−2 circumstance penalty**; weapons with the nonlethal trait (including your fists) skip that penalty.

And the payoff is automatic — **Getting Knocked Out** (*Player Core* p. 410): if the damage came from a nonlethal attack, the target **doesn't gain the dying condition**; they're simply unconscious at 0 HP. No bleed-out, no recovery checks, nothing to stabilize. The rules hand you a live prisoner for free.
